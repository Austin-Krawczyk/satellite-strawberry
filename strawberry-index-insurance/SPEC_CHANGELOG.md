- 2026-09-15 — §6 Step 1 unit rule, provisional: default changed from "≥ 60% strawberry in both CDL 2022 and 2023" to "≥ 60% in CDL 2023". The both-years rule was meant to filter crop rotation but is filtering CDL 2022 misclassification: of pixels CDL 2023 labels Strawberries, CDL 2022 labels 32% Shrubland and 16% Grass/Pasture, which is not plausible rotation on intensively farmed coastal land; forward agreement (CDL 2022 strawberry → 2023 strawberry) is 90.5%, and only 2 cells were ≥ 60% strawberry in 2022 but not 2023, so real rotation is low. The 15-unit both-years set is kept as a sensitivity check to report in the exhibit. Final rule to be chosen once the user supplies the March 2023 flood extent reference, from unit counts inside and outside the footprint under both rules. Approved by user.

- 2026-09-16 — §5 CIMIS endpoints replaced: the legacy CIMIS Web API (`/api/data`, `/api/station`, key as the `appKey` query parameter) was operational only through 2026-07-31 and now returns a site-firewall rejection page for any request carrying `appKey`, whatever its value. - 2026-09-16 — Step 4b result, spatial coherence: the spread IS coherent. Mean absolute difference between station pairs is 13.7 mm within 15 km against 42.0 mm beyond 50 km, a factor of 3.1; nearest neighbours mostly agree closely (Arroyo Seco and Soledad II 3.1 mm at 13 km, the two Salinas stations 3.4 mm at 17 km, Gilroy and Pajaro 0.9 mm at 22 km). Station totals range 33.8 to 137.3 mm across the study area, a factor of four, against four distinct CPC values and the same 79.2 mm at eight of fourteen stations. The grid argument is therefore about resolution, and §8 item 8 now says a denser network in the producing districts would resolve real structure. The station spread is carried into §9 beside the five-distinct-values finding as the strongest evidence for the grid argument, because it is ground measurement rather than products disagreeing with each other.
- 2026-09-16 — CORRECTION before publication: the claim that the coarse products read low along the coast does NOT survive testing across stations. Across 28 station-years at all 14 stations, CPC's median is 123% of the station total and it reads more than 15% low at only 5 of 28; the rank correlation of CPC against distance to the coast is +0.33, i.e. it reads relatively higher inland, not lower at the coast. The real pattern is regression to the cell mean: the coarse products under-read the wettest sites (De Laveaga 53% in 2022) and over-read the driest (Arroyo Seco 214% in 2022, 291% in 2023). The single-station reading in the first draft of §5.3 was one station, not a coastal bias, and has been replaced.
